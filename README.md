# Plumbline

> And the LORD said unto me, Amos, what seest thou? And I said, A plumbline.
> Then said the Lord, Behold, I will set a plumbline in the midst of my people
> Israel…
>
> — **Amos 7:8**

**Plumbline** is a free Bible app: read the text, study it down to the word,
memorize it, sing from the hymnal, and hand it to someone else. Everything runs
on your device and works offline. No account, no sign-in, no telemetry; your
notes and study data stay local and private.

It ships on the web and on Android, in nine languages — interface and scripture
both. It follows your device's language, and you can pick one under Settings.
Every text is public domain:

| Language | Bible |
|---|---|
| English | King James Version (1769) |
| German | Luther 1912 |
| Spanish | Reina-Valera 1909 |
| Arabic | Smith & Van Dyck |
| Punjabi | Free Bibles India |
| Hindi | Free Bibles India |
| French | Ostervald |
| Traditional and Simplified Chinese | Chinese Union Version (1919) |

Word-level study (the Strong's dictionary, concordance, grammar) is available
where the text carries word tags — English, German, and Spanish today. The
other languages are reader editions: reading, search, notes, tags, threads,
weaves, memorization, and sharing all work the same.

## Features

### Reading

- Passages side by side, in a browser or on a phone
- Parallel passages annotated across side-by-side chapters
- Reopens exactly where you left off, mid-chapter included
- Book → chapter → verse tap-grid navigator, marking where you have been recently
- Theme, text size, alignment, and font settings
- Optional plain-English overlay (AKJV) for easier reading
- A canon strip under the reader showing where you are in the Bible

![Acts 7 and Genesis 15 side by side in a deep indigo theme, weave connectors
between the quoted passages, and the weave's own note with its cited verses
in the study panel — the same reader, re-inked by a
theme](assets/readme/themeable.png)

### Study

- Tap any word for its dictionary entry, every rendering of the underlying
  word, and a concordance of where it appears
- The same root traced across the Old and New Testaments
- The 1769 translators' own margin notes
- Cross-references from the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge
- Search that handles partial matches, phrases, and original-language words
- Optional statistical layers — where a word concentrates, which words keep
  company — all computed on your device, all off by default

### Your own work

- Personal notes on any verse, marked in the gutter
- Tags, with a picker that offers what you already have
- Threads: a passage list built over time, with a note per entry
- Weaves: your own verse-to-verse link graph, with notes
- Convert a tag into a weave as you study
- Visualizations of your weaves: a chord diagram across the canon, and a
  constellation with pinnable lanes
- A notes browser over everything you have written

### Practice

- Spaced-repetition memorization (SM-2) for verses or whole passages, with
  three drills: first letters, progressive blank-out, typed recall scored word
  by word
- A reading map: coverage across the canon, an activity heatmap, and a history
  log; chapters you have not visited lately glow
- Mark a chapter or book read by hand, on a date you pick
- Hymnal: public-domain hymns with chords the engine transposes, per-language
  texts, and a sing mode that scrolls continuously

### Sharing

- Present mode: a thread as a fullscreen, high-contrast, large-type
  presentation, with tap-to-focus and an "in context" fade
- Share is a QR code that puts the passage on the other person's phone
- Your church's details (time, location, website) travel with every link you
  share, and land as a button in the receiver's top bar

### The application

- Free. No account, no Play Store, no paid tier. Works offline.
- Every bit of your data backs up to a zip and restores on any device.

## Getting started

First launch asks who's opening the Book — **new in the faith** (a welcome
with next steps, landing in John), **curious about the Bible** (for someone
still deciding what they believe), **sharing the gospel** (straight into the
Romans Road presentation), or **established believer** (set up study and
memorization, and choose how much study detail sits beside the text). All of
it is switchable any time in **Settings**; the text and your own notes, tags,
and threads are always on. Whichever welcome you were given stays one tap
away afterwards, under **Welcome** in the top bar.

Then:

1. **Read.** Tap the passage button (`John 3 ▾`) for the book → chapter →
   verse navigator, swipe (or `←`/`→`) to step chapters.
2. **Tap a word** (double-click on desktop) — the study panel opens: your
   note, the word's occurrences, and the dictionary entry.
3. **Long-press a verse** (right-click on desktop) — copy, share, write a
   note, **tag** it, add it to a thread, or memorize it.
4. **Tag as you go, weave later.** Tag passages by topic over weeks; open the
   tag and hit **⇔ make weave** to chain them through the canon. Point two
   panes at linked passages and the connectors draw themselves.
5. **≡ menu** holds the rest: Memorize, Explore, History, Present, the guide,
   and Settings — including **backup to a zip** that restores on any device.

## Share the Bible — and your church — with one code

Put your church in **Settings → Your church** (name, when and where you meet,
your website) and it travels with everything you share. **Share** in the top
bar gives a QR and a link that carry both: whoever scans it gets the whole
Bible, offline and free, *and* your church's details saved on their device —
with a **Church** button in their top bar that opens your website. If they
pass the app on, your church goes with it.

Hand a card across at a service, print the code on a bulletin, or text the
link. Nothing is registered anywhere: the details ride in the link itself
(`?church=…`), so you can read exactly what you are sending before you send
it, and there is no account or server involved on either end.

Sharing from **Present** is the same code with one difference: it opens for
someone meeting the Bible rather than setting up a study tool, offering just
*new in the faith* or *curious about the Bible*. Turn that off in Settings if
you'd rather it behave like an ordinary share.

## Threads and weaves

**A weave is something you find. A thread is something you make.**

Weaves are already in the Bible: Matthew quoting Isaiah, one event told in
three Gospels, a Psalm on the lips of Christ at the cross. A weave writes down
the connection you noticed, and from then on the reader draws it whenever
either end is open — no mode to enter. A verse scrolled out of view leaves its
connector pinned at the pane edge as a hint. Two views map the library:
**Map** (book-to-book density as chord ribbons) and **Constellation** (one
lane per weave along the canon).

A thread is yours: a set of passages you gathered in an order that makes your
point — Sunday's sermon, the case you want to walk a friend through. The
Romans Road is a thread. Threads are walked start to finish and carry your
notes as they go.

> [!NOTE]
> The weaves shipped with the app began life as **AI-generated study aids**. The
> 28 in the library arrive with every link marked `approved`, and the flag is
> surfaced in the reader; anything sitting in **Suggested** (under Explore) is
> still a proposal until you approve it against the text yourself.

## Your data

Your notes, tags, threads, weaves, reading history and memory work are yours, and
they stay on your device. **Settings → Back up (.zip)** exports all of it, and
**Restore from backup…** loads it on any device — the archive layout is shared
between the phone and the browser, so a phone backup restores in the browser
and vice versa.

## Data provenance

The KJV (public domain) comes from eBible.org's Strong's-tagged SWORD module;
Strong's from Open Scriptures (CC-BY-SA); morphology from OSHB (CC-BY 4.0) and
Robinson's public-domain Textus Receptus parsing; cross-references from the
Treasury of Scripture Knowledge via openbible.info. The other Bibles: Luther
1912 via The Unbound Bible with Zefania Strong's tags, Reina-Valera 1909 and
Smith & Van Dyck 1865 via eBible.org, the Ostervald and both editions of the
1919 Chinese Union Version via open-bibles, and the Punjabi and Hindi Bibles
from Free Bibles India (CC BY-SA 4.0) — the rest public domain. The German and
Spanish dictionary definitions are machine translations of Strong's, and say
so in the app. Scripture renders in EB Garamond (OFL), with per-script faces
(Amiri, Noto Serif) bundled the same way. Full credits and licenses, file by
file: **[BIBLIOGRAPHY.md](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md)**.

## Translation selection

This application is built with the 1769 King James Version of the Bible, which
translates the Masoretic Hebrew texts and the Textus Receptus. This is the same
version that has been used for hundreds of years in English and has seen
millions of souls saved and lives transformed. I argue that it is also the only
version in English that satisfies the Bible's own promises about the
preservation of Scripture (Psalm 12:6–7; Psalm 119:89, 152, 160; Proverbs
30:5–6; Isaiah 40:8; Matthew 5:18; Matthew 24:35; Luke 21:33; 1 Peter 1:23–25;
Revelation 22:18–19).

versions that so many churches use today. Below is a sampling of why that is
